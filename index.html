Our heritage

A living tradition, passed from Acharya to Acharya

For more than three hundred years, the family at Vylongara Kalari has preserved the knowledge of Mashinottam and Jyothisham, handing it down through six generations of one family. Today the fifth-generation Acharya leads the Kalari, and the sixth generation is learning beside her.

What began as a family practice beside the Kshetram near Chumanna Mannu has become a place where devotees from across Kerala and beyond come for guidance, prayer and peace of mind.

“Every generation receives the tradition as it was given, and keeps it as it was kept.”

Questions

Frequently asked

What is Mashinottam?

Mashinottam is a traditional Kerala practice of seeking guidance through observation of mashi (sacred ink). At Vylongara Kalari it has been practised by the same family for six generations. The present Acharya is of the fifth generation, and the sixth is learning the tradition under her guidance.

What are the temple timings?

The nada opens 5:00 – 9:30 AM and 5:00 – 8:30 PM. Deeparadhana is at 6:45 PM. See the Nithya Pooja page for the full daily schedule.

Do I need to book before visiting?

Yes. Please book on WhatsApp at least 2 days ahead. On your booked date you may come any time between 10 AM and 4 PM; devotees are seen in order of arrival.

How much does a consultation cost?

There is no fixed fee for a direct consultation. A ₹101 booking token confirms your visit, and you may offer any dakshina you wish on the day. Online phone consultations are ₹1,001.

Can I consult from abroad or another city?

Yes. Choose an online consultation on WhatsApp. The Acharya calls you on a normal phone call between 7 PM and 10 PM in the time slot you book.

Which details are needed for a booking?

Name, age, house name, star (nakshatra) and place of the person for whom the consultation or pooja is booked.

Are payments refundable?

Booking tokens, consultation fees and pooja payments are non-refundable. Please see our Cancellation & Refund Policy for the exceptions.
